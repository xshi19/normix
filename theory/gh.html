The Generalized Hyperbolic Distribution#

This page states the generalized hyperbolic (GH) formulas that normix computes. The marginal integral, the scale orbit, the natural-parameter expansion, and the Bessel form of the joint Hellinger distance are derived in the GH note. The family was introduced by BarndorffNielsen1977.

normix writes a density as \(f(x\mid\theta)\). On that site the same function is \(p_\theta(x)\).

Definition#

Let \(Y\) be GIG with parameters \((p, a, b)\), and let \(Z\) be an independent Gaussian vector with mean zero and covariance \(\Sigma\). Then

(1)#\[X \stackrel{d}{=} \mu + \gamma Y + \sqrt{Y} Z\]

is generalized hyperbolic with parameters \((\mu, \gamma, \Sigma, p, a, b)\). Here \(\mu, \gamma \in \mathbb{R}^d\) and \(\Sigma\) is positive definite. \(\Sigma\) is the covariance of \(Z\). The constructor from_classical() takes \((\mu, \gamma, \Sigma, p, a, b)\) and stores the Cholesky factor of \(\Sigma\).

  • \(\mu\): location

  • \(\gamma\): skewness

  • \(\Sigma\): dispersion

  • \((p, a, b)\): GIG parameters of \(Y\)

Any law of the form (1), for a nonnegative \(Y\), is a normal mixture in normix.

Note

In many references, “normal mixture” means a discrete mixture of normal densities. In normix it means a random vector that can be written as (1).

Joint density#

The joint law of \((X, Y)\) is the joint GH distribution:

(2)#\[\begin{split}f(x, y \mid \mu, \gamma, \Sigma, p, a, b) &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^d |\Sigma|}} \frac{(a/b)^{p/2}}{2 K_p(\sqrt{ab})} y^{p - 1 - d/2} \\ &\quad \times \exp\left( -\frac{1}{2}(x - \mu - \gamma y)^\top \Sigma^{-1} (x - \mu - \gamma y) y^{-1} - \frac{1}{2}(b y^{-1} + a y) \right),\end{split}\]

for \(y > 0\).

Marginal density#

Integrating out \(y\) gives the marginal density. The calculation is in the GH note. The density that log_prob() evaluates is

(3)#\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} f(x \mid \mu, \gamma, \Sigma, p, a, b) &= (2\pi)^{-d/2}\,|\Sigma|^{-1/2}\, \frac{(a/b)^{p/2}}{K_p(\sqrt{ab})} \left(\frac{A}{Q+b}\right)^{(d/2-p)/2} \\ &\quad \times K_{p-d/2}\!\left(\sqrt{A(Q+b)}\right) \exp\!\left(\gamma^\top\Sigma^{-1}(x-\mu)\right). \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

where \(Q = (x-\mu)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(x-\mu)\) and \(A = a + \gamma^\top\Sigma^{-1}\gamma\).

The conditional law used by the E-step is

\[Y \mid X = x \sim \mathrm{GIG}\!\left(p - \tfrac{d}{2},\; a + \gamma^\top\Sigma^{-1}\gamma,\; b + Q\right).\]

Parameters and scale#

For every \(c > 0\) the parameter sets

\[(\mu,\; \gamma,\; \Sigma,\; p,\; a,\; b) \quad\text{and}\quad (\mu,\; \gamma/c,\; \Sigma/c,\; p,\; a/c,\; bc)\]

give the same marginal law of \(X\). The product \(ab\) is invariant, so the concentration \(\omega = \sqrt{ab}\) is invariant, while the scale \(\delta = \sqrt{b/a}\) is multiplied by \(c\). The Fisher information of the unrestricted parameter is singular. The derivation of the orbit is in the GH note.

Two representatives are implemented:

  • regularize_a_eq_b() sets \(a = b = \omega\).

  • regularize_det_sigma_one() sets \(|\Sigma| = 1\), the constraint in McNeil2010. regularize_det_sigma() sets \(\log|\Sigma|\) to a chosen target.

Since \(|\Sigma/c| = |\Sigma|/c^d\), a small change in the matrix scale moves \(|\Sigma|\) sharply when \(d\) is large. Fixing \(|\Sigma| = 1\) does not control the condition number of \(\Sigma\). The mechanisms are described in EM Framework. Other normalizations used in the literature are in the GH note.

Moments#

(4)#\[\begin{split}E[X] &= \mu + \gamma\,E[Y], \\ \mathrm{Cov}[X] &= E[Y]\,\Sigma + \mathrm{Var}[Y]\,\gamma\gamma^\top.\end{split}\]

These identities use the moments of \(Y\) that they display. When \(\gamma_i = 0\), the drift terms vanish, so \(E[X_i] = \mu_i\) as soon as \(E[\sqrt{Y}] < \infty\), and \(\mathrm{Cov}[X] = E[Y]\,\Sigma\) needs only \(E[Y] < \infty\). For an inverse-gamma subordinator (the Student-\(t\) case \(\nu = 2\alpha\)) that is \(\alpha > 1/2\) for the mean and \(\alpha > 1\) for the covariance. When \(\gamma_i \ne 0\) the mean needs \(E[Y]\) and the covariance needs \(\mathrm{Var}[Y]\). Cauchy (\(\alpha = 1/2\), \(\gamma = 0\)) has no mean: the integral is \(+\infty\), not \(\mu\). A gamma subordinator has every positive moment, so variance-gamma does not inherit the inverse-gamma table. A zero coordinate of \(\gamma\) is not multiplied by an infinite moment of \(Y\).

Conditioning on \(Y\) gives the third and fourth central moments. The expansion is in the GH note. For coordinate \(i\), with \(m_k = E[Y^k]\) from (2) or from the boundary law,

(5)#\[\begin{split}\mu_3(X_i) &= \gamma_i^3\,\mu_3(Y) + 3\,\gamma_i\,\Sigma_{ii}\,\mathrm{Var}[Y], \\ \mu_4(X_i) &= \gamma_i^4\,\mu_4(Y) + 6\,\gamma_i^2\,\Sigma_{ii}\,E[(Y-m_1)^2 Y] + 3\,\Sigma_{ii}^2\,E[Y^2].\end{split}\]

Skewness is \(\mu_3/\sigma^3\) and excess kurtosis is \(\mu_4/\sigma^4 - 3\). When \(\gamma = 0\) the skewness is \(0\) and the excess kurtosis is \(3\,E[Y^2]/E[Y]^2 - 3\), which equals \(3/\alpha\) for variance-gamma and \(3/(\alpha-2)\) for normal-inverse gamma (\(\alpha > 2\)). The \(t_3\) law (normal-inverse gamma, \(\gamma = 0\), \(\alpha = 1.5\)) has no fourth moment, so the excess kurtosis is \(+\infty\). When \(\gamma \ne 0\), the term \(\gamma_i^4\mu_4(Y)\) requires \(E[Y^4] < \infty\) (inverse-gamma shape \(\alpha > 4\)).

skewness() and kurtosis() return these values, component-wise for \(d > 1\).

Exponential family#

The joint law (2) is an exponential family

\[f(x, y\mid\theta) = h(x, y) \exp\left(\theta^\top t(x, y) - \psi(\theta)\right).\]

Expanding the joint exponent produces the coordinates below. The expansion is in the GH note.

\[\begin{split}t(x, y) = \begin{pmatrix} \log y \\ y^{-1} \\ y \\ x \\ x y^{-1} \\ x x^\top y^{-1} \end{pmatrix}, \qquad h(x, y) = (2\pi)^{-d/2}\,\mathbf{1}_{y > 0}.\end{split}\]

The blocks have sizes \(1\), \(1\), \(1\), \(d\), \(d\), and \(d \times d\). In the parameter array the last block is stored as a vector of length \(d^2\): the coefficient of \(\mathrm{vec}(xx^\top y^{-1})\) is \(-\tfrac12\mathrm{vec}(\Sigma^{-1})\).

(6)#\[\begin{split}\theta_1 &= p - 1 - \frac{d}{2}, \\ \theta_2 &= -\frac{1}{2}\left(b + \mu^\top \Sigma^{-1} \mu\right), \\ \theta_3 &= -\frac{1}{2}\left(a + \gamma^\top \Sigma^{-1} \gamma\right), \\ \theta_4 &= \Sigma^{-1} \gamma, \\ \theta_5 &= \Sigma^{-1} \mu, \\ \theta_6 &= -\frac{1}{2} \Sigma^{-1}.\end{split}\]
(7)#\[\psi(\theta) = \frac{1}{2} \log|\Sigma| + \log 2 + \log K_p(\sqrt{ab}) + \frac{p}{2} \log\left(\frac{b}{a}\right) + \mu^\top \Sigma^{-1} \gamma.\]
(8)#\[\begin{split}\eta_1 &:= E[\log Y] = \left.\frac{\partial}{\partial r} \left(\sqrt{\frac{b}{a}}\right)^r \frac{K_{p+r}(\sqrt{ab})}{K_p(\sqrt{ab})} \right|_{r=0}, \\ \eta_2 &:= E[Y^{-1}] = \sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} \frac{K_{p-1}(\sqrt{ab})}{K_p(\sqrt{ab})}, \\ \eta_3 &:= E[Y] = \sqrt{\frac{b}{a}} \frac{K_{p+1}(\sqrt{ab})}{K_p(\sqrt{ab})}, \\ \eta_4 &:= E[X] = \mu + \gamma \eta_3, \\ \eta_5 &:= E[X Y^{-1}] = \mu \eta_2 + \gamma, \\ \eta_6 &:= E[X X^\top Y^{-1}] = \Sigma + \mu \mu^\top \eta_2 + \gamma \gamma^\top \eta_3 + \mu \gamma^\top + \gamma \mu^\top.\end{split}\]

Here \(\eta = \nabla\psi(\theta) = E[t(X, Y)]\). The first three coordinates are the GIG expectation parameters of \(Y\). On a variance-gamma, normal-inverse-gamma, or normal-inverse-Gaussian joint, the same block is returned with the gamma, inverse-gamma, or inverse-Gaussian moments of \(Y\). Those entries are not the partial derivatives of \(\psi\) after the frozen coordinate (\(b = 0\), \(a = 0\), or \(p = -1/2\)) has been removed. Where a moment of \(Y\) does not exist, the entry is \(+\infty\).

M-step#

(9)#\[\begin{split}\mu &= \frac{\eta_4 - \eta_3 \eta_5}{1 - \eta_2 \eta_3}, \\ \gamma &= \frac{\eta_5 - \eta_2 \eta_4}{1 - \eta_2 \eta_3}, \\ \Sigma &= \eta_6 - \eta_5 \mu^\top - \mu \eta_5^\top + \eta_2 \mu \mu^\top - \eta_3 \gamma \gamma^\top, \\ (p, a, b) &= \arg\max_{p,a,b} L_{\text{GIG}}(p, a, b \mid \eta_1, \eta_2, \eta_3),\end{split}\]

where \(L_{\text{GIG}}\) is (7). These are the updates m_step() applies, with the expectations in (8) replaced by conditional expectations. The derivation is in the GH note. The EM write-up is EM Algorithm for Generalized Hyperbolic Distributions.

Hellinger distance#

There is no closed form asserted here for the Hellinger distance between two marginal GH laws. For two joint laws with natural parameters \(\theta\) and \(\varphi\), squared_hellinger() evaluates

\[H_{\mathrm{JGH}}^2 = 1 - \exp\left( \psi\left(\frac{\theta+\varphi}{2}\right) - \frac{\psi(\theta)+\psi(\varphi)}{2} \right).\]

The average is in natural coordinates. When the normal parameters agree, this reduces to the GIG Hellinger distance. Marginalization gives \(H_X^2 \le H_{\mathrm{JGH}}^2\). The marginal method returns the joint distance, which is an upper bound on the marginal distance. The Bessel-ratio expansion and the comparison are derived in the GH note (Shi2016).

Special cases#

  • Normal-inverse Gaussian (BarndorffNielsen1997): \(p = -1/2\), class NormalInverseGaussian.

  • Variance-gamma (MadanSeneta1990): \(b \to 0\), class VarianceGamma.

  • Normal-inverse gamma: \(a \to 0\), class NormalInverseGamma.

  • Student-\(t\): \(p < 0\), \(a = 0\), \(\gamma = 0\).

  • Hyperbolic: order \(p = 1\). This is not a separate class.
