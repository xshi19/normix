EM Algorithm for Generalized Hyperbolic Distributions#

This page states the EM formulas that normix computes for a generalized hyperbolic mixture: the posterior moments, the averaged E-step, the M-step map, the scale normalization, the ECM cycles, and the mixing updates for variance-gamma, normal-inverse Gaussian, and normal-inverse gamma. The complete-data likelihood, the equivariance argument, and the likelihood inequality are derived in the EM note. The algorithm is due to Dempster1977. The GH specialization follows Hu2005.

normix writes a density as \(f(x\mid\theta)\). On that site the same function is \(p_\theta(x)\).

The EM note writes the classical tuple \((\mu, \gamma, \Sigma, p, a, b)\) as \(\vartheta\). This page uses \(\theta\) for the natural parameter of the joint family, as in The Generalized Hyperbolic Distribution. The expectation parameter is \(\eta = \nabla\psi(\theta)\).

See also: executable walkthroughs in Batch EM in practice and EM vs MCECM Algorithm Comparison.

Conditional distribution#

Let \(X\) follow (1), with \(Y \sim \mathrm{GIG}(p, a, b)\) and \(\Sigma\) positive definite. For \(a > 0\) and \(b > 0\),

\[Y \mid X = x \sim \mathrm{GIG}\!\left( p - \frac{d}{2},\; a + \gamma^\top\Sigma^{-1}\gamma,\; b + q(x)\right),\]

where \(q(x) = (x-\mu)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(x-\mu)\). conditional_expectations() evaluates the posterior moments of this law. The expansion of the joint density is in the EM note.

Conditional moments#

(1)#\[E[Y^\alpha \mid X = x] = \left( \sqrt{\frac{b + q(x)}{a + \gamma^\top\Sigma^{-1}\gamma}} \right)^\alpha \frac{K_{p - d/2 + \alpha}\!\left(\sqrt{(b + q(x))(a + \tilde q)}\right)} {K_{p - d/2}\!\left(\sqrt{(b + q(x))(a + \tilde q)}\right)},\]

where \(\tilde q = \gamma^\top\Sigma^{-1}\gamma\). This is (2) at the posterior parameters. The log moment is the derivative at \(\alpha = 0\). With \(A = a + \tilde q\) and \(B = b + q(x)\),

\[E[\log Y \mid X = x] = \tfrac12\log(B/A) + \left.\partial_\nu\log K_\nu(\sqrt{AB})\right|_{\nu = p - d/2}.\]

On a variance-gamma, normal-inverse-Gaussian, or normal-inverse-gamma model the same ratio is evaluated at the posterior parameters in the sections below.

E-step#

Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_n \in \mathbb{R}^d\). e_step() returns the averages below, stored on NormalMixtureEta:

(2)#\[\begin{split}\hat s_1^{(k)} &= \frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n E[Y^{-1}\mid X = x_j], \\ \hat s_2^{(k)} &= \frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n E[Y\mid X = x_j], \\ \hat s_3^{(k)} &= \frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n E[\log Y\mid X = x_j], \\ \hat s_4^{(k)} &= \frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n x_j, \\ \hat s_5^{(k)} &= \frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n x_j\, E[Y^{-1}\mid X = x_j], \\ \hat s_6^{(k)} &= \frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n x_j x_j^\top E[Y^{-1}\mid X = x_j].\end{split}\]

The conditional moments are (1). The joint sufficient statistic in (8) is ordered \((\log Y,\, Y^{-1},\, Y,\, X,\, XY^{-1},\, XX^\top Y^{-1})\). The EM note uses that order for \(\widehat\eta\). This page keeps the stored order (2). The GIG solver receives \((\hat s_3^{(k)}, \hat s_1^{(k)}, \hat s_2^{(k)})\). The reason for these six blocks is in the EM note.

M-step#

For a full exponential family the M-step maximizes \(\langle\theta, \eta\rangle - \psi(\theta)\), with \(\eta\) in the sufficient-statistic order of (8). The Bregman form of that inversion is in the exponential-family note. m_step() calls from_expectation() and applies

(3)#\[\begin{split}\mu_{k+1} &= \frac{\hat s_4^{(k)} - \hat s_2^{(k)}\hat s_5^{(k)}} {1 - \hat s_1^{(k)}\hat s_2^{(k)}}, \\ \gamma_{k+1} &= \frac{\hat s_5^{(k)} - \hat s_1^{(k)}\hat s_4^{(k)}} {1 - \hat s_1^{(k)}\hat s_2^{(k)}}, \\ \Sigma_{k+1} &= \hat s_6^{(k)} - \hat s_5^{(k)}\mu_{k+1}^\top - \mu_{k+1}(\hat s_5^{(k)})^\top + \hat s_1^{(k)}\mu_{k+1}\mu_{k+1}^\top - \hat s_2^{(k)}\gamma_{k+1}\gamma_{k+1}^\top, \\ (p_{k+1}, a_{k+1}, b_{k+1}) &= \arg\max_{p,a,b} L_{\mathrm{GIG}}(p, a, b \mid \hat s_3^{(k)}, \hat s_1^{(k)}, \hat s_2^{(k)}),\end{split}\]

where \(L_{\mathrm{GIG}}\) is (7). In the order of (9), \((\eta_1,\eta_2,\eta_3,\eta_4,\eta_5,\eta_6) = (\hat s_3^{(k)}, \hat s_1^{(k)}, \hat s_2^{(k)}, \hat s_4^{(k)}, \hat s_5^{(k)}, \hat s_6^{(k)})\).

A non-degenerate mixing law has \(\hat s_1^{(k)}\hat s_2^{(k)} > 1\). The recovered \(\Sigma_{k+1}\) is the covariance of a normal law, so it has to be positive definite. The mixing maximizer has to lie in the subordinator’s domain. For generalized hyperbolic that domain is

\[\hat s_2^{(k)} > 0,\quad \hat s_1^{(k)} > 0,\quad \hat s_1^{(k)}\hat s_2^{(k)} > 1,\quad -\log\hat s_1^{(k)} < \hat s_3^{(k)} < \log\hat s_2^{(k)}.\]

These bounds are necessary. Equality is a point-mass boundary, and the inequalities do not guarantee an interior generalized hyperbolic law.

m_step_normal() updates only \((\mu, \gamma, \Sigma)\). m_step_subordinator() updates only the mixing law. from_expectation() is the three-parameter inversion. The algebra of (3) is in the EM note.

Scale normalization#

For every \(s > 0\) the parameter sets

\[(\mu,\;\gamma,\;\Sigma,\;p,\;a,\;b) \quad\text{and}\quad (\mu,\;\gamma/s,\;\Sigma/s,\;p,\;a/s,\;bs)\]

give the same marginal law of \(X\). The map is the replacement \(Y \mapsto sY\). The concentration \(\omega = \sqrt{ab}\) is invariant, and the scale \(\delta = \sqrt{b/a}\) is multiplied by \(s\). The Fisher information \(I(\theta) = \nabla^2\psi(\theta)\) is singular on this orbit. The equivariance argument is in the EM note. The same orbit is stated in The Generalized Hyperbolic Distribution.

regularize_det_sigma() chooses \(s = \exp((\log|\Sigma| - \tau)/d)\) and applies the map. regularize_det_sigma_one() is the case \(\tau = 0\), so \(|\Sigma| = 1\) and \(s = |\Sigma|^{1/d}\):

\[(\mu, \gamma, \Sigma, p, a, b) \mapsto \left(\mu,\; |\Sigma|^{-1/d}\gamma,\; |\Sigma|^{-1/d}\Sigma,\; p,\; |\Sigma|^{-1/d}a,\; |\Sigma|^{1/d}b\right).\]

The mixing parameters travel with \(s\) as follows.

  • Generalized hyperbolic: \((a, b) \mapsto (a/s,\, bs)\), and \(p\) is fixed. regularize_a_eq_b() uses \(s = \sqrt{a/b}\), which sets \(a = b = \omega\).

  • Variance-gamma, with rate \(\beta\): \((\alpha, \beta) \mapsto (\alpha,\, \beta/s)\).

  • Normal-inverse gamma, with rate \(\beta\): \((\alpha, \beta) \mapsto (\alpha,\, s\beta)\).

  • Normal-inverse Gaussian: \((\mu_{IG}, \lambda) \mapsto (s\mu_{IG},\, s\lambda)\). regularize_a_eq_b() uses \(s = 1/\mu_{IG}\), which sets \(\mu_{IG} = 1\).

Variance-gamma and normal-inverse gamma leave regularize_a_eq_b() unchanged, because one GIG rate is zero. On a factor model the same \(s\) acts by \(F \mapsto F/\sqrt{s}\) and \(D \mapsto D/s\), through regularize_det_sigma().

ECM cycles#

BatchEMFitter runs one of two cycles. Multi-cycle ECM (MCECM) is the conditional-maximization scheme of MengRubin1993, as used for GH mixtures in McNeil2010.

  • algorithm='em': E-step, then (3), then the selected regularization.

  • algorithm='mcecm': E-step, then m_step_normal(), then the selected regularization, then a second E-step, then m_step_subordinator().

The regularization is none (the default), det_sigma_one, det_sigma_x, or a_eq_b. det_sigma_one applies the coupled map above, including \(\gamma\) and the mixing parameters. When a statistic rule is set, it is applied to the first E-step average only. The fitter subtracts the sample mean from the data and from \(\mu\) before these updates, and adds that mean back to \(\mu\). \(\gamma\), \(\Sigma\), and the mixing parameters are unchanged by the shift. A shrinkage target equal to the initial model’s expectation parameters is expressed in the centered coordinates. The penalty itself is Shrinkage with Penalized Likelihood.

The ascent argument for an exact conditional maximization is in the EM note. An increase of the complete-data objective does not by itself select a global maximum of the observed likelihood. The fitter loop is described in EM Framework.

Special mixing families#

The normal block remains (3). Only the mixing update changes. The three boundary fits are summarized in the EM note. The docstrings of Gamma and InverseGamma write the digamma as \(\psi\). On this page and in the EM note, \(\psi_0\) is the digamma and \(\psi_1 = \psi_0'\) is the trigamma, so \(\psi\) remains the log-partition. The shared Newton step, for a scalar target, is

\[\alpha \leftarrow \alpha - \frac{\psi_0(\alpha) - \log\alpha - \mathrm{target}} {\psi_1(\alpha) - 1/\alpha}.\]

Variance-gamma#

\(Y \sim \mathrm{Gamma}(\alpha, \beta)\) with rate \(\beta\), the limit \(b \to 0\) (MadanSeneta1990). The moments used by the update are

\[E[\log Y] = \psi_0(\alpha) - \log\beta, \qquad E[Y] = \alpha/\beta.\]

from_expectation() receives \((\hat s_3^{(k)}, \hat s_2^{(k)})\) and solves

\[\psi_0(\alpha) - \log\alpha = \hat s_3^{(k)} - \log\hat s_2^{(k)}, \qquad \beta = \alpha/\hat s_2^{(k)}.\]

The target in the Newton step is \(\hat s_3^{(k)} - \log\hat s_2^{(k)}\). A finite gamma moment satisfies \(\hat s_2^{(k)} > 0\) and \(\hat s_3^{(k)} < \log\hat s_2^{(k)}\). The posterior used by the E-step is

\[Y \mid X = x \sim \mathrm{GIG}\!\left( \alpha - \tfrac{d}{2},\; 2\beta + \gamma^\top\Sigma^{-1}\gamma,\; q(x)\right).\]

The case \(q(x) = 0\) is discussed in the EM note.

Normal-inverse Gaussian#

\(Y \sim \mathrm{IG}(\mu_{IG}, \lambda)\), the case \(p = -1/2\) (BarndorffNielsen1997). The moments are

\[E[Y] = \mu_{IG}, \qquad E[Y^{-1}] = 1/\mu_{IG} + 1/\lambda.\]

from_expectation() receives \((\hat s_2^{(k)}, \hat s_1^{(k)})\) and returns

\[\mu_{IG} = \hat s_2^{(k)}, \qquad \lambda = \frac{1}{\hat s_1^{(k)} - 1/\hat s_2^{(k)}},\]

which requires \(\hat s_2^{(k)} > 0\) and \(\hat s_1^{(k)} > 1/\hat s_2^{(k)}\). The posterior is GIG of order \(-1/2 - d/2\), with rates \(\lambda/\mu_{IG}^2 + \gamma^\top\Sigma^{-1}\gamma\) and \(\lambda + q(x)\). The closed form is in the EM note.

Normal-inverse gamma#

\(Y \sim \mathrm{InvGamma}(\alpha, \beta)\) with rate \(\beta\), the limit \(a \to 0\). The moments used by the update are

\[E[\log Y] = \log\beta - \psi_0(\alpha), \qquad E[Y^{-1}] = \alpha/\beta.\]

from_expectation() receives \((-\hat s_1^{(k)}, \hat s_3^{(k)})\) and solves

\[\psi_0(\alpha) - \log\alpha = -\hat s_3^{(k)} - \log\hat s_1^{(k)}, \qquad \beta = \alpha/\hat s_1^{(k)}.\]

The target in the Newton step is \(-\hat s_3^{(k)} - \log\hat s_1^{(k)}\). A finite inverse-gamma moment of this pair satisfies \(\hat s_1^{(k)} > 0\) and \(\hat s_3^{(k)} > -\log\hat s_1^{(k)}\). The posterior used by the E-step is

\[Y \mid X = x \sim \mathrm{GIG}\!\left( -\alpha - \tfrac{d}{2},\; \gamma^\top\Sigma^{-1}\gamma,\; 2\beta + q(x)\right).\]

The scalar solve is in the EM note.

Numerical remarks#

The conditioning of \(\Sigma_{k+1}\) in (3) is the subject of Shrinkage with Penalized Likelihood. Overflow of the posterior Bessel ratio, and the distinction between an exact \(Q\) increase and a global maximum, are discussed in the EM note. Direct maximization of the observed likelihood is compared in Why not gradient descent and the gradient-descent note.
