Entropy, Varentropy, and Rényi Entropy#

This page states the entropy, varentropy, and Rényi formulas that normix computes for exponential families and for joint normal variance-mean mixtures. The escort differentiation, the \(\chi^2\) decomposition, and the general base-measure covariance formula are derived in the varentropy note. For the structural properties of varentropy see Stankyavichyus2026.

normix writes a density as \(f(x\mid\theta)\). On that site the same function is \(p_\theta(x)\).

The surprisal is \(\mathcal{I}(X) = -\log f(X\mid\theta)\). The Fisher information is \(I(\theta) = \nabla^2\psi(\theta)\). These are different objects: \(\mathcal{I}(X)\) is a random variable, and \(I(\theta)\) is a matrix.

See also: Monte Carlo checks in Entropy, varentropy, and fat tails.

Definitions#

Let \(X\) have density \(f\) with respect to a reference measure \(\mu(dx)\).

(1)#\[H = \mathbb{E}[-\log f(X)], \qquad V_H = \operatorname{Var}[-\log f(X)].\]

\(H\) is the differential entropy and \(V_H\) is the varentropy. The varentropy needs only \(\mathcal{I}(X) \in L^2\), so it stays finite for laws whose fourth moment diverges.

The Rényi entropy of order \(\alpha > 0\), \(\alpha \ne 1\) (Renyi1961), is

(2)#\[H_\alpha = \frac{1}{1-\alpha} \log \int f(x)^\alpha \, \mu(dx),\]

and \(H_\alpha \to H\) as \(\alpha \to 1\).

Density power#

(3)#\[R(\alpha) = \log \int f(x)^\alpha \, \mu(dx), \qquad R(1) = 0.\]

After the reflection \(\alpha \mapsto 1-\alpha\), \(R\) is the cumulant-generating function of the surprisal, so

(4)#\[H = -R'(1), \qquad V_H = R''(1),\]

and \(H_\alpha = R(\alpha)/(1-\alpha)\). The expansion

\[H_\alpha = H - \tfrac{1}{2} V_H\,(\alpha - 1) + \mathcal{O}\!\left((\alpha-1)^2\right)\]

is the slope of the Rényi spectrum at \(\alpha = 1\). The cumulant-generator identification is in the varentropy note. entropy(), varentropy(), and renyi() differentiate log_density_power().

Exponential family#

For

\[f(x\mid\theta) = h(x)\exp\{\theta^\top t(x) - \psi(\theta)\}\]

with \(\log h \equiv b_0\) on the support,

(5)#\[R(\alpha) = (\alpha - 1)\,b_0 + \psi(\alpha\theta) - \alpha\,\psi(\theta),\]

when \(\alpha\theta\) lies in the natural domain. Otherwise \(R(\alpha) = +\infty\), and \(H_\alpha = R(\alpha)/(1-\alpha)\) inherits the sign of \(1-\alpha\). For a gamma law of shape \(s\) the condition is \(\alpha > 0\) and \(\alpha(s-1)+1 > 0\). For an inverse-gamma law of shape \(s\) it is \(\alpha(s+1)-1 > 0\), which already forces \(\alpha > 0\).

With \(\eta = \nabla\psi(\theta)\),

(6)#\[H = \psi(\theta) - \theta^\top \eta - b_0, \qquad V_H = \theta^\top I(\theta)\,\theta, \qquad H_\alpha = \frac{(\alpha-1)\,b_0 + \psi(\alpha\theta) - \alpha\,\psi(\theta)}{1-\alpha}.\]

The quadratic form holds because \(\mathcal{I}(X) - H = -\theta^\top\{t(X) - \eta\}\). This covers Gamma, InverseGamma, and GeneralizedInverseGaussian (\(b_0 = 0\)), and MultivariateNormal (\(\log h \equiv 0\), \(V_H = d/2\)).

Note

InverseGaussian has \(\log h(x) = -\tfrac12\log(2\pi) - \tfrac32\log x\), which is not constant. The general covariance correction is in the varentropy note. normix evaluates the inverse-Gaussian quantities on the embedding \(\mathrm{GIG}(-\tfrac12, \lambda/\mu^2, \lambda)\), where \(\log h \equiv 0\) and \(\log x\) is a sufficient statistic.

GIG#

Let \(Y \sim \mathrm{GIG}(p, a, b)\) with density (1) and \(\theta = (p-1,\,-b/2,\,-a/2)\). Substituting the GIG log-partition into (5) gives, with \(z = \sqrt{ab}\),

(7)#\[R(\alpha) = (1-\alpha)\log 2 + \frac{1-\alpha}{2}\log\!\frac{b}{a} + \log K_{1 + \alpha(p-1)}(\alpha z) - \alpha \log K_p(z).\]

On the math site \(\sqrt{ab}\) is the concentration \(\omega\), and the operator below is written \(\mathcal{L}\). Here \(z = \omega\) and

\[L = (p-1)\,\partial_p + z\,\partial_z.\]

Differentiating along the escort path produces

(8)#\[\boxed{\; V_H\{\mathrm{GIG}(p,a,b)\} = (L^2 - L)\log K_p(z), \qquad z = \sqrt{ab}. \;}\]

Expanded, this is

\[V_H = (p-1)^2 \partial_p^2 \log K_p(z) + 2(p-1)\,z\,\partial_p\partial_z \log K_p(z) + z^2 \partial_z^2 \log K_p(z),\]

which equals \(\theta^\top I(\theta)\,\theta\). The entropy is

(9)#\[H\{\mathrm{GIG}(p,a,b)\} = \log\{2 K_p(z)\} + \tfrac12\log\!\frac{b}{a} - L\log K_p(z).\]

For \(a, b > 0\) every moment of \(Y\) is finite, and \(V_H\) stays finite, including in the inverse-gamma limit \(a \to 0\) where the kurtosis diverges. The tail comparison is in the GIG note.

Joint law#

For the joint law (2), conditionally on \(Y\), the quadratic form in the Gaussian exponent is \(\chi^2_d\) and independent of \(Y\). That decomposition is in the varentropy note. It gives

(10)#\[\boxed{\; V_H(X, Y) = \frac{d}{2} + \operatorname{Var}\!\left[\mathcal{I}_Y + \frac{d}{2}\log Y\right], \;}\]

where \(\mathcal{I}_Y = -\log f_Y(Y)\) is the subordinator surprisal. The Gaussian layer contributes \(d/2\). The parameters \(\mu\), \(\gamma\), and \(\Sigma\) change the joint entropy only through constants and means. They drop out of \(V_H\) except through the dimension \(d\).

For a GIG subordinator, \(t(Y) = (\log Y,\, Y^{-1},\, Y)\) and \(\mathcal{I}_Y = \psi_{\mathrm{GIG}}(\theta) - \theta^\top t(Y)\), so

\[\mathcal{I}_Y + \tfrac{d}{2}\log Y = \psi_{\mathrm{GIG}}(\theta) - \left(\theta - \left(\tfrac{d}{2},\, 0,\, 0\right)^\top\right)^\top t(Y).\]

The variance of this shifted statistic is

(11)#\[\boxed{\; V_H(X, Y) = \frac{d}{2} + (L_d^2 - L_d)\log K_p(z), \qquad L_d = \left(p - 1 - \tfrac{d}{2}\right)\partial_p + z\,\partial_z, \qquad z = \sqrt{ab}. \;}\]

For \(d = 0\) this is (8). The shift \(p - 1 \mapsto p - 1 - d/2\) is the natural coordinate \(\theta_1\) in (6). Variance-gamma (\(b \to 0\)) and normal-inverse gamma (\(a \to 0\)) evaluate \(R(\alpha)\) from their own subordinator log-partitions. The normal-inverse Gaussian joint fixes \(p = -1/2\) in its log-partition, so the density-power integral is evaluated on the GH embedding, where \(p\) moves with \(\alpha\).
